# One-page concept brief

## Audience and problem

Research, investing, procurement, strategy, and content teams repeatedly narrow a noisy field into a consequential shortlist. Their criteria often drift across spreadsheets, conversations, and reviewers; scores hide disqualifiers; and final rankings lose the evidence that produced them.

## Positioning

GemSieve is filter intelligence for defensible shortlists: explicit gates, weighted signals, evidence, uncertainty, and reasons in one reusable pass. It augments human judgment rather than presenting a black-box recommendation.

## Information architecture

The home page explains the promise and four-step pass. Product defines the sieve anatomy. Use cases translate it into four buyer workflows. Waitlist and pilot pages capture distinct intent. Policy pages state data, product-stage, and metaphor boundaries.

## Visual direction

An original “signal laboratory” system: dark forest field, acid chartreuse signal, warm paper, hard rules, graph-paper texture, and instrument-like labels. The composition is Decide/Learn, not a dashboard pretending to contain live customer data.

## Calls to action and revenue hypothesis

Primary conversion: consented early-access signup. Higher-intent conversion: qualified pilot request. Initial revenue hypothesis is paid pilot design and onboarding, followed by recurring team software. No prices or availability are claimed before CRO/CTO approval.
